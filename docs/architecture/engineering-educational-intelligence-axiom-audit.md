# Axiom Audit Trail — Engineering Educational Intelligence

Full validation pass applied to the 12 candidate Foundational Axioms proposed in the master blueprint's first axiom draft, prior to freezing. Six tests applied to each: Irreducibility, Independence, Universality, Domain Specificity, Architectural Necessity, Non-Ideological grounding. Verdicts: PASS / PASS WITH REWORDING / MERGE / SPLIT / DEMOTE TO PRINCIPLE / REMOVE. Result: 12 candidates → 10 frozen axioms. See `engineering-educational-intelligence-blueprint.md` §FA for the finalized, reworded axiom text — this document records *why* each verdict was reached.

---

**A1 — Latency of Learning → PASS WITH REWORDING (merged with A2, see below).**
Passes irreducibility (nothing else implies it), independence, architectural necessity (a system that treats the mark as the learning fails by the book's own definition of correctness). Fails a strict reading of domain specificity — "latent state, evidenced indirectly" is structurally identical to medicine (symptoms→disease) and credit scoring (behavior→creditworthiness); it is borrowed epistemology, not invented here. Reworded to anchor the domain-specific necessity in *how wide the gap is* (no instrument here approaches a biomarker's directness) and *how severe collapsing it is* (institutional treatment of a developing person), rather than claiming the abstract principle itself is novel.

**A2 — Proxy–Goal Divergence → MERGE into A1.**
This is Goodhart's/Campbell's Law, a domain-agnostic economics principle — the canonical example (Soviet nail-factory quotas) has nothing to do with education. It fails domain specificity outright. It also fails irreducibility once the derivation path is traced: accept A1 (signal ≠ construct) plus the well-established external fact that optimization pressure exploits gaps between measure and target, and A2 follows necessarily — it doesn't need to stand as an independent law. It remains fully load-bearing (every reasoning engine must still respect it) as A1's stated corollary, not as a sibling axiom.

**A3 — Bounded, Derived Confidence → PASS WITH REWORDING.**
Independent of A1 (a fully evidence-based, traceable system could still assign arbitrary confidence numbers — calibration and traceability are different failures) and of A7/Traceable Derivation (traceability tells you a claim has an origin; this governs whether its *strength* is honestly proportioned to that origin). Fails domain specificity in the abstract (Bayesian calibration is universal); earns necessity because miscalibration here reshapes a developing person's institutional trajectory, not just a forecast's accuracy record. Reworded to make that stakes-tie explicit rather than presenting the axiom as free-floating statistical hygiene.

**A4 — Trajectory over Snapshot → PASS (cleanest pass in the set).**
Here domain specificity actually holds up under scrutiny: in most domains longitudinal tracking is merely useful, but in education the learner's actual *purpose* in the system is to change over time — growth is the stated goal, not incidental context. One caveat added under the non-ideological test: a licensing exam can legitimately gate on a point-in-time threshold by institutional design; this axiom governs the system's internal representation of the learner, not a claim that every institutional outcome must itself be trajectory-based.

**A5 — Causal Interpretation → PASS WITH REWORDING.**
Independent of A1 (accepting the evidence/truth gap doesn't by itself force causal modeling — many systems accept the gap and still only correlate) and of stakeholder plurality (a single-user AI tutor with no other stakeholders still needs to interpret cause to act correctly). Fails abstract domain specificity (identical structure in medicine and fraud detection); necessity sharpened by tying it explicitly to intervention-targeting stakes — a misattributed cause sends a real remediation resource to the wrong problem.

**A6 — Evidentiary Immutability → PASS WITH REWORDING.**
Tested against its most plausible competitor — mutable records plus a change-log — and survives independently: that alternative can superficially satisfy traceability while still losing the falsifiability guarantee immutability provides (a change-log documents that something changed; it doesn't preserve the original evidence in a form later computations can still reference). Load-bearing for both A2 (confidence needs a stable evidence base to be reproducible) and A7 (traceable derivation), which is why it survives as its own axiom rather than being read as a mere technique implementing the stakes axiom (A10, final numbering).

**A7 — Traceable Derivation → PASS WITH REWORDING.**
Checked for collapse into A3/Bounded Confidence — distinct, since a black-box model can be well-calibrated while remaining untraceable. Checked against accountability (formerly A10, now a corollary of the final Asymmetric Stakes axiom) — this axiom is what makes accountability *substantive* rather than nominal (a named responsible person with no way to inspect why a system said what it said is accountable in name only). That relationship is stated explicitly as reinforcement, not treated as a derivation, since nominal accountability could technically exist without it.

**A8 — Structured Knowledge → PASS WITH REWORDING.**
The universality test surfaced a genuine counterexample: some domains (flashcard-style corporate compliance training) are closer to flat lists than hierarchies. Resolved by scoping the claim honestly rather than overclaiming universality: a flat domain is a degenerate case of a graph with no edges, not a violation. Remains necessary wherever real prerequisite structure exists, which covers the overwhelming majority of curricula this book addresses.

**A9 — Inherent Plurality → SPLIT.**
The instruction was explicit: check whether stakeholder access and institutional context are one fact or two, and do not preserve elegance at the expense of correctness. The homeschooling case settles it: one tutor, one parent, one student have full stakeholder plurality with zero institutional structure — proving plurality doesn't presuppose institution. Conversely, a solo teacher's private gradebook used inside a real school can nominally satisfy plurality while ignoring the school's binding policies, resourcing limits, and culture — proving institutional embeddedness is independently violable. Split into two axioms (Stakeholder Plurality; Institutional Embeddedness), with the latter also noted as a precondition for the accountability corollary (formerly A10) to be expressible at all — an accountable "role" only exists because an institution defines it.

**A10 — Human Locus of Accountability → MERGE into the final Asymmetric Stakes axiom.**
An honest engineering argument was constructed (not an ethical appeal): a wrong high-stakes automated decision has no valid in-architecture correction path — "ask the same algorithm again" produces the same answer — so a human override point is what makes such a decision *bounded-correctable* at all. This passes architectural necessity. It fails domain specificity outright: identical reasoning governs lending, parole-scoring, and medical triage. It also fails the non-ideological test on close inspection — reasonable engineers genuinely disagree about *where* the automation boundary sits (fully automated content sequencing is defensible; fully automated academic tracking is not), which means the precise boundary is a judgment call, not an invariant. What is invariant is narrower: given this domain's stakes (the final axiom's root claim), *some* decisions need a human locus. That narrower claim merges cleanly as a corollary; the boundary question is explicitly left to 4.2 as a design principle.

**A11 — Asymmetric Ethical Weight → retained, becomes the final axiom's root (renumbered Axiom 10).**
Passes irreducibility (root claim, nothing else implies it), universality, and architectural necessity. Domain specificity holds up better than most: while "lifelong record, compounding stakes" is shared with a few other domains (medical records, criminal records), education's distinctive angle is that the record is made about a person *during the years their identity and opportunity structure are being formed* — a wrong medical record about a settled adult and a wrong "low-capability" label about a 12-year-old have different developmental-harm profiles. Reworded to make this developmental framing explicit rather than resting on the more generic "sensitive lifelong data" claim alone.

**A12 — Fragile, Asymmetric Trust → MERGE into the final Asymmetric Stakes axiom.**
Aggressively challenged per instruction: is trust-repair-as-distinct-from-data-repair unique to education, or shared with all safety-critical/socio-technical systems? Honestly, shared — a misdiagnosis shapes a patient's self-perception even after correction; a wrong parole score shapes community treatment even after correction. This is established socio-technical-trust research, not an education-specific discovery, and does not survive a uniqueness claim under domain specificity. It is, however, a necessary corollary once the root stakes claim (A11) is accepted, and it is the reason the Five Failure Questions discipline runs through every section rather than living in one optional chapter. Merged in as the second named corollary of the final axiom, alongside accountability.

---

## Result

12 candidates → **10 frozen axioms**. Nothing of substance was deleted: A2, A10, and A12 were reattributed as necessary corollaries of their true parent axiom (A1 and the final Asymmetric Stakes axiom respectively) rather than losing status entirely, and A9 was correctly split rather than artificially merged. See the blueprint's §FA for the final, reworded, numbered set (Axioms 1–10).
