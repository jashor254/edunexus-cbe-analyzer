# Production #001 Storyboard Bible — "Why ¾ Is Bigger Than ⅗"

**Status:** Educational storyboard logic — the learner's journey from misconception to understanding, translated into frame-level visual reasoning. No Manim code, no SVG, no artwork, no drawn frames. Every frame below is specified in words: what it shows conceptually, why it must exist, and what it does to the learner's thinking — the contract animation departments implement, not the implementation itself.

**Built on the locked script** ([Script Lab](edunexus-production-001-script-lab.md)) and the staging corrections already directed in the [Master Blueprint](edunexus-production-001-blueprint.md) (Section 4's memory-target rebalancing). Where the Table Read flagged a script line as packing two ideas into one sentence (lines 3, 10, 14), this storyboard resolves it at the *frame* level — splitting the visual beat across two frames sharing one continuous piece of narration — without touching the locked script text itself. This is a legitimate storyboarding technique, not a script edit, and it closes that Table Read finding at this layer instead of leaving it pending.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This storyboard is built from the learner's thinking, not from the script's sentences — the two are related but not identical, and the difference matters. A single locked line of narration sometimes needs two distinct visual beats (the wrong guess, then the reasoning behind it); a pause sometimes needs its own frame with zero new content, because the frame's entire job is to let something already shown finish landing. The result is nineteen frames, each tested against one question: does removing this frame make the learner's understanding measurably weaker? One candidate frame ("Let's look," a pure narrative transition) failed that test and was cut before it ever entered the inventory — recorded in Section 10, not silently dropped.

The single most consequential finding in this document is the correction already directed by the Master Blueprint, now made concrete at the frame level: Frame 15, not Frame 9, must carry the strongest stillness, the most generous negative space, and the longest silence in the entire piece — because Frame 15 is where the actual memory target lands, and Frame 9 (the earlier, more visually satisfying "three quarters is bigger" reveal) was found, by real evidence from the Table Read, to be competing for that role. This document is where that correction becomes a specific, checkable instruction for whoever builds the animation, not just a stated intention.

## 2. Learner Thinking Map

Before any frame, the mind being moved through this production, at each major beat.

| Beat | Current belief | New evidence introduced | Mental conflict created | Reasoning performed | Updated understanding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orientation/Question | None yet — forming a first impression | The two fractions, named | None yet | A quick, intuitive guess forms | A committed prediction now exists to be tested |
| Expectation | "Three fifths is bigger — 5 is more than 4" | None — this is the belief stated back | None yet | Recognizes their own reasoning | Confirms the guess feels reasonable, not careless |
| Observation | Still holds the wrong belief | Two identical wholes, cut differently, three pieces shaded in each | Building, not yet triggered | Visually comparing the two shaded regions | Notices the shaded regions are not equal |
| Gap | Belief now directly contradicted | The stated result: ¾ is bigger | Full contradiction — "5 is bigger, so how is 3/5 smaller?" | Actively searching for what could explain this | Genuine confusion, motivated to find out why |
| Investigation | Belief broken, no replacement yet | What's held constant vs. what varies between the two wholes | Resolving | Isolating cause: the cut count is the only real difference | Beginning to see the mechanism |
| Recognition | Old belief replaced by a specific causal statement | "Four cuts, bigger pieces. Five cuts, smaller pieces." | Resolved for this pair | Connecting the specific case to a general principle | Understands *why*, not just *that* |
| Meaning | Holds the general mechanism | The denominator redefined: partition count, not quantity | None — consolidating | Generalizing beyond this one pair | The memory target: "more cuts, smaller pieces" |
| Reflection | Holds the general rule | None — deliberate pause | None | Letting the rule settle | Consolidation, no new content |
| Transfer | Holds the general rule, untested | A new, unseen pair (⅔ vs. 2/7) | A fresh, smaller-scale version of the original test | Applying the rule independently | Confirms the rule transfers, not just recalls |

**The storyboard follows this map, not the sentence structure of the script** — which is exactly why some single sentences become two frames, and one narrative connector ("Let's look") becomes no frame at all.

## 5. Transition Logic

Why the learner is ready to move at each cut, why the next frame exists, and what would go wrong if it were skipped.

| Transition | Why the learner is ready | Why the next frame exists | Cognitive burden if skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| F01 → F02 | Both objects are named and settled | A prediction can't be tested without first being made | Skipping to evidence without a stated prediction removes the Gap's entire mechanism (Story Engine §8) |
| F02 → F03 | A prediction now exists | The wrong answer needs to be stated back, not assumed | Without naming the likely wrong guess, the later correction targets no one specifically |
| F03 → F04 | The *what* is stated | The *why* needs its own beat, not folded into the same breath | Combining them is exactly the packed-sentence risk the Table Read flagged |
| F04 → F05 | The reasoning is out in the open | It needs to be normalized before the contradiction lands, or the contradiction reads as a judgment | Skipping this risks the emotional-safety failure the Pattern Library's Correction pattern explicitly warns against |
| F05 → F06 | The learner is ready to look at real evidence | Evidence has to be built, not asserted | Asserting "¾ is bigger" without showing the wholes first violates the Evidence-First Mandate at the frame level |
| F06 → F07 | The first whole is fully understood | The second whole must be visible *alongside* the first for comparison, not shown alone | Sequential replacement rather than side-by-side display would make direct visual comparison impossible |
| F07 → F08 | Both wholes are visible | The comparison itself needs uninterrupted silence to actually register | Narrating over this moment does the learner's looking *for* them, weakening the realization when it comes |
| F08 → F09 | The learner has already seen the answer with their own eyes | The result needs to be stated plainly, confirming what was seen | Without a stated resolution, an uncertain viewer might not trust their own read of the visual |
| F09 → F10 | The contradiction is now fully formed | The confusion needs to be voiced, not silently resolved by the viewer alone | A viewer who quietly resolves the tension without the piece naming it skips the productive confusion that motivates real Investigation |
| F10 → F11 | The learner is actively searching for an explanation | Attention needs to be directed at what's constant first | Jumping straight to "what varies" without first establishing "what's constant" removes half the causal isolation |
| F11 → F12 | The constant is identified | The variable needs its own distinct highlight | Combining both highlights in one frame is the second packed-sentence risk the Table Read flagged |
| F12 → F13 | Both constant and variable are isolated | The causal relationship needs to be stated explicitly, not left implied | Without this frame, the learner has correlation (things differ) but not causation (why it matters) |
| F13 → F14 | The specific causal fact is understood | It needs to be generalized beyond this one pair, or nothing transfers | Skipping the generalization leaves the learner with a fact about ¾ and ⅗ specifically, not a rule |
| F14 → F15 | The general mechanism is understood | It needs a dedicated, maximally protected frame to actually consolidate as the memory target | Without a frame this deliberately isolated, the memory target competes for attention with the frame that immediately precedes it |
| F15 → F16 | The rule is consolidated | A genuinely new test is required to confirm it transfers, not just that it was heard | Without a new test case, the piece cannot honestly claim the Transfer Test (Story Engine §10) has been satisfied |
| F16 → F17 | The new case is on screen | The operative concept (same numerator) needs to be reconnected before the question is posed | Asking the question without the callback risks the learner guessing rather than reasoning |
| F17 → F18 | A genuine thinking pause has occurred | The question needs a stated resolution, or it's a broken promise | An unresolved question at this late stage undermines trust in the piece's own structure |
| F18 → F19 | The core claim is fully delivered | Only if the Council retains it — this frame is not required for the core claim to land | Skipping it costs nothing to the core understanding, per Section 10's honest finding below |

## 6. Cognitive Load Review

| Frame(s) | Load level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| F01–F05 | Low | Orientation and prediction — deliberately light, per Pattern Library §7, so full capacity is available for the Investigation cluster later |
| F06–F09 | Low–Medium | Evidence-building and the Gap — real content, but visually simple and directly perceptual, not analytical |
| F10 | Low | A single, focused question — no new visual complexity |
| **F11–F13** | **Medium–High (the piece's only High-load cluster)** | The actual Investigation — Pattern Library §7 permits this, and only this cluster, to carry the heaviest load; protected by dedicated pacing and two separate highlight frames rather than one packed one |
| F14–F15 | Medium, settling to Low | Generalization and consolidation — load decreases deliberately as the piece moves toward its memory target, matching the Visual Blueprint's "simplify at Resolution" rule |
| F16–F18 | Low–Medium | Transfer — real thinking required, but scaffolded by the repeated visual grammar from F06–F07, reducing new-format load |
| F19 | Low | No new conceptual content |

**Helpful visual support:** the repeated visual grammar between F06–F07 and F16 (Section 4) reduces load at the transfer stage by letting the *format* feel familiar even though the *numbers* are new. **Thinking pauses:** F02 (prediction), F08 (comparison), F15 (consolidation), F17 (transfer question) — four genuine pauses, none redundant with each other, each protecting a different kind of thinking. **Moments requiring silence specifically:** F08 and F15 — the only two frames with zero narration, and per Section 4's specification, F15's silence must run longer than F08's. **Working-memory demand peaks** at F11–F13, exactly where the Pattern Library's own load rules say it's allowed to.

## 7. Memory Target Analysis

**The exact frame where the permanent realization occurs: F15.** Everything from F01 through F14 prepares it; F16 through F19 reinforce it by testing whether it actually transfers.

**Does any other frame accidentally compete with it?** This was the single most important finding across the entire prior review chain (Table Read → Decision Log DL-009 → Master Blueprint §4), and this storyboard is where it gets a final, concrete check: **F09** (the original "¾ is bigger" reveal) was the identified competitor — shorter, more concrete, arriving earlier, and previously staged with the strongest silent beat in the piece. This document's Section 4 specification directly resolves it: F09 is now explicitly instructed to be clear but *not* the visual superlative, while F15 is explicitly instructed to carry the longest pause and most generous negative space in the entire production. No other frame was found to compete — F08's silence (the comparison beat) is real but shorter and precedes the actual resolution, so it doesn't compete with F15 for the same *conclusion*-shaped memory slot the way F09 did.

**Residual risk, stated honestly:** this is a staging correction, not yet a tested one. Whether F15 actually wins the recall competition against F09 in practice can only be confirmed by the Post-Publication comprehension check already planned in the Master Blueprint (§11) — this document closes the *design* gap, not the *evidence* gap.

## 8. Misconception Audit

Checking every frame for whether the wrong belief could survive it, and why.

- **F01–F05:** the misconception is fully alive here **by design** — these frames exist specifically to state and normalize it before it's challenged. This is correct, not a hidden failure.
- **F06–F08:** the misconception is still technically uncontradicted in the learner's stated belief, though the evidence contradicting it is now on screen — this is the intended tension point, resolved deliberately at F09, not before.
- **F09–F10:** the misconception is broken but **not yet replaced** — a learner could, at this exact point, accept "¾ is bigger" as a fact about this one pair without understanding why, which is precisely why F11–F14 exist and cannot be skipped or compressed.
- **F11–F14:** the misconception is actively being replaced with the correct mechanism — by F14, no viewer should still hold the original belief in its general form, though they may not yet have generalized the new rule fully.
- **F15:** the misconception should be fully replaced by this point — if a learner reaches F15 still believing "bigger denominator, bigger fraction" in general, F11–F14 did not do their job, and this is exactly the failure the Learning Gate's real comprehension check (Master Blueprint §10) exists to catch.
- **F16–F18:** this is the actual test of whether the misconception is truly gone — a learner who still holds it, even partially, will likely answer the transfer question incorrectly, which is diagnostic, not just confirmatory.
- **No frame after F15 was found to reintroduce ambiguity** about what a denominator means — the repeated visual grammar in F16 deliberately reuses the *corrected* framing, never reverting to language or imagery that could be read as endorsing the original misconception.

## 9. Accessibility Review

- **Caption timing:** every frame's narration-bearing beats (all except F08 and F15, which are silent by design) require captions synced exactly to speech, per the Visual Language Standard §10 — no frame currently requires text to arrive ahead of or behind its narration.
- **Colour independence — a genuinely positive finding:** the core distinction driving the entire piece (four pieces vs. five pieces) is carried by **shape and count**, not colour — colour is only introduced at F14 as reinforcement of an already-established mechanism, never as the sole carrier of a distinction a colourblind viewer would otherwise miss. This production is close to colour-independent by the nature of its own content, not by a late accommodation.
- **Shape recognition:** the two wholes must remain clearly distinguishable by cut count alone (four visible divisions vs. five) at any screen size, including a small phone screen — this is a hard requirement for F06–F09 and F16 specifically, since the entire Gap depends on the viewer being able to actually count the pieces.
- **Reading burden:** on-screen text is minimal throughout (fraction symbols, brief highlight labels) — no frame requires sustained reading, consistent with the Visual Language Standard's rule that text echoes speech rather than running a parallel track.
- **Viewing on phones:** the two-whole comparison (F06–F09, F16) is the single highest-risk frame cluster for small-screen legibility — piece boundaries must remain crisp and countable at typical phone-video scale, flagged here as a specific animation-department requirement, not a general aspiration.
- **Viewing without audio:** F08 and F15's silence is already caption-free by design (nothing is said), but every other frame's core content must be fully recoverable from captions alone — a viewer with no audio should still be able to follow the Expectation → Gap → Investigation → Resolution arc from text and visuals alone.
- **Viewing without colour:** confirmed low-risk per the colour-independence finding above — F14's colour introduction reinforces an already-visually-established mechanism and carries no information a greyscale viewer would miss.

## 10. Editorial Cuts

Applying the strict test — "if this frame disappeared, would understanding decrease?" — to every candidate, including two that don't survive it unqualified.

- **"Let's look" (script line 5) — cut before entering the frame inventory.** This line is a pure narrative connector with no distinct visual content of its own; it was folded into the lead-in of F06 rather than given its own Frame ID, since giving it a standalone frame would have meant a frame existing for pacing alone, not for a change in thinking.
- **F05 (reassurance) — flagged, not cut, on an honest technicality.** Applying the strict test literally: removing F05 does not reduce the learner's *mathematical* understanding — the mechanism is taught identically either way. F05's actual job is protecting trust and emotional safety (Section 4), not advancing understanding directly. This is the same category of tension the Table Read surfaced around script line 16 — a real value the frame provides that isn't quite "understanding" in the strict sense this section tests for. **Recommendation: keep F05**, but this document declines to silently pass it as meeting the strict test unqualified — the Editorial Standards Council should be aware this frame is justified on trust grounds, not comprehension grounds, consistent with how the Table Read handled the analogous line 16 tension rather than quietly resolving it either way.
- **F19 — already conditional, not newly cut.** Per Section 4, this frame's inclusion is pending the same Council ruling already identified in the Master Blueprint; the strict test here returns a clean "no, understanding would not decrease without it," which is exactly why its inclusion was never treated as settled.
- **No other frame in the inventory failed the test.** Every remaining frame, checked individually, was found to either directly advance understanding or protect a specific, named risk already identified in a prior review pass (F05's trust function, F08 and F15's silence functions).

## 12. Storyboard Readiness Verdict

**READY FOR ANIMATION AS AN ENGINEERING TASK — with one frame's inclusion still pending the same Editorial Standards Council ruling already identified in the Master Blueprint, and one frame's justification (F05) explicitly logged as trust-based rather than comprehension-based.**

Every frame in this inventory traces to a specific point on the Learner Thinking Map (Section 2), passes the strict removal test in Section 10 except where honestly flagged otherwise, and the one genuinely open risk carried forward from prior review — the memory-target staging conflict — has been resolved here with a specific, checkable instruction (F15 as the piece's visual superlative, F09 explicitly not) rather than left as an intention for animators to interpret. The two remaining open items are both already-known, already-tracked decisions (F19's inclusion, F05's honest justification) — neither requires reopening this storyboard, and neither should be allowed to delay animation from beginning against everything else specified here.
