# The EduNexus House Voice — Casting Document

**Status:** Casting brief and audition framework. No voice has been cloned, no TTS installed, no speech generated. This document operationalizes the [House Voice Standard](edunexus-house-voice-standard.md) into a real casting process — the difference between defining what the narrator should sound like and actually finding the human being who sounds like that.

**Relationship to the House Voice Standard:** that document is the fixed identity — psychological profile, acoustic targets, forbidden registers, the listening test. This document does not re-derive any of it; it applies it to the specific, practical question the Standard deliberately deferred: *who, specifically, gets cast, how do we know when we've found them, and only then, which technology reproduces them faithfully.*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Casting the EduNexus House Voice is not a search for the best-sounding voice available. It is a search for one specific person whose natural, unforced way of speaking already matches the identity the House Voice Standard describes — because that identity was built to describe a real kind of person (the teacher every student secretly prefers, per that Standard's own Section 3), not an abstract vocal specification invented independently of any human being. The casting process below exists to find that person, capture a reference recording of them that a synthesis system can faithfully learn from, and only then evaluate which technology can carry that specific identity forward for the next twenty years without eroding it.

The central discipline this document holds, echoed in every "DO NOT" at the top of the brief that spawned it: **technology is evaluated last, against a real recording, not first, against a spec sheet.** A studio that picks XTTS or ElevenLabs before it has cast a real voice is optimizing for what's easy to synthesize, not for what's actually trustworthy — exactly backwards from what a permanent brand asset requires.

---

## 2. Ideal Narrator Profile — The Person

Forgetting microphones and models entirely: this is a Kenyan man or woman in their early-to-mid thirties, most plausibly someone with real classroom experience — a teacher, a former teacher, or someone whose family and community regularly turn to them to explain something complicated calmly. Not a broadcaster by trade, not a performer, not someone whose voice is already a public brand elsewhere (a well-known radio or TV personality would import an existing set of associations EduNexus doesn't want to inherit). Someone whose natural speaking voice, in an ordinary conversation, already has the qualities the House Voice Standard describes — because those qualities were never meant to be *acted*, they were meant to be *found*.

**Why learners trust them:** because nothing about the way they speak asks to be trusted — no vocal effort spent trying to sound impressive, confident, or exciting. Trust here is earned the way it's earned from a favourite teacher: consistently, over time, by never once overselling, never once talking down, and always taking the time to make sure the explanation actually lands before moving on.

**Why teachers listen:** because the register is recognisably one of their own — a colleague's voice, not an outside expert's or a marketer's. A veteran teacher hearing this narrator should feel spoken *alongside*, not lectured *at*.

**Why parents feel comfortable:** because the voice never rushes past a hard topic and never manufactures alarm around one either — a parent hearing this narrator explain something concerning about their child should feel informed, not frightened, and should feel the narrator understands exactly how much weight that moment carries for them.

**What makes them believable, in one sentence:** they sound like they would say the same thing, in the same way, whether or not anyone was listening — no register-switching between "explaining to a child" and "presenting to an official," because the respect underneath is identical in both cases.

## 3. Voiceprint Specification

Every parameter below restates the House Voice Standard's acoustic profile (§5) in measurable, audition-checkable form, with the learning rationale made explicit for each.

| Parameter | Target | Why it improves learning |
|---|---|---|
| **Estimated age impression** | Early-to-mid 30s | Old enough for unforced authority; young enough to sound genuinely curious rather than settled — a listener trusts explanation more from someone who still sounds interested in the subject |
| **Pitch range** | Mid register, natural, unforced | Extremes (very low or very high) read as performed; a natural mid pitch reads as unmediated, ordinary speech, which is what sustains trust over years of listening |
| **Resonance / chest voice** | Moderate, present but not dominant | Enough warmth to carry on a phone speaker without needing bass-heavy playback — most EduNexus content is heard on exactly that kind of device |
| **Brightness** | Mid-low | Bright, forward voices read as commercial; a warmer, less forward brightness supports long-form listening without fatigue (House Voice Standard §11) |
| **Texture** | Smooth with light natural grain | Perfectly smooth reads as synthetic even before any synthesis is involved; light natural grain is what makes a voice sound like a specific person rather than a category of voice |
| **Warmth** | High, present as a baseline, never spiking | A listener should never be able to point to the "warm part" of the narration — it should be uniformly present, which is what makes it feel sincere rather than performed at specific moments |
| **Breathiness** | Minimal, natural | Enough to sound human; excess breathiness reads as intimate/informal in a way that's wrong for an authority register a parent needs to trust with real stakes |
| **Projection** | Conversational-close | This is a voice speaking to one listener through a screen, not addressing a room — over-projection breaks the one-to-one relationship the House Voice Standard's Section 3 says the narrator must hold |
| **Speaking energy** | Moderate-low, steady | High energy is exhausting to listen to at length and signals performance; steady moderate energy is what sustains the Listening Test's 30-minute standard (House Voice Standard §11) |
| **Cadence / rhythm** | Natural prose rhythm, variable phrase length | A metronomic or sing-song cadence signals reading rather than explaining; natural variance signals genuine, in-the-moment thought |
| **Silence** | Comfortable with pause, unhurried to fill it | A narrator uncomfortable with silence rushes, which directly damages the thinking-time function pauses serve throughout every EduNexus production (Story Engine §6, §11) |
| **Sentence endings** | Falling, settled, complete | Uptalk (rising terminal pitch) reads as uncertain — wrong for a narrator whose authority must be quiet but total |
| **Emphasis** | On meaning, not adjectives; one stressed word per sentence at most | Over-emphasis is a hallmark of persuasive/advertising registers; restrained emphasis keeps attention on content, not delivery |
| **Breathing pattern** | Audible, natural, positioned at real phrase boundaries | Perfectly edited/removed breath sounds read as synthetic; natural breath at the right places is one of the strongest signals of a real, unforced human speaker |
| **Reading speed** | 140–155 words per minute baseline | Below typical ad-read pace; matches the House Voice Standard's unhurried target and supports comprehension for a Grade 7 audience specifically, the platform's most demanding listener |

**Audition-time check:** every parameter above should be checkable from a two-minute unscripted conversation with the candidate before any scripted reading is attempted — a voice that only achieves this profile when reading prepared copy, and loses it in natural conversation, is being performed, not possessed, and should be treated with real caution (see Section 8).

## 4. East African English Specification

Extending the House Voice Standard's own treatment (its §7) with casting-specific detail.

**What should remain, unapologetically:** natural Kenyan English vowel quality, consonant patterns, and sentence rhythm — the full, ordinary register of an educated Kenyan professional speaking to a general Kenyan audience, the same register a respected local news anchor or university lecturer uses. This is not a dialed-down or softened accent; it is the accent, present at its natural strength.

**What should be neutral:** pronunciation choices that would specifically favour one Kenyan sub-region, ethnic community, or social register over another — the target is the broadly shared, broadcast-standard Kenyan English register that already functions as a national lingua franca in professional and educational contexts, not a marked regional or class-coded variant.

**What should never disappear, under any circumstance including future re-casting or re-synthesis:** natural stress and intonation patterns distinct from British or American English; correct, unhesitant, warm pronunciation of Kenyan names, places, and Swahili loan-words — given this platform names real children by name in its output, a narrator who hesitates or anglicizes a learner's own name is a direct, tangible trust failure, not a stylistic quibble.

**How Kenyan English should influence narration without limiting future regional understanding:** by staying at the level of a genuinely national broadcast register rather than a hyper-local one — the same design choice that lets a Kenyan newsreader be immediately, comfortably understood in Kampala or Dar es Salaam without either erasing their Kenyan-ness or requiring listeners elsewhere to adjust. This is the register that gives the platform room to grow regionally (per the Visual Language Standard's own noted expansion horizon) without requiring the voice itself to change.

## 5. Trust Analysis

**Once:** immediate credibility on tone alone — calm, unhurried delivery signals "this isn't trying to sell me anything" within the first few seconds, before any content has even registered.

**Ten times:** recognition begins — a listener starts to notice the voice is *always* like this, not just this once, which is the first real evidence of consistency, the specific quality a one-off charismatic voice cannot produce.

**One hundred times:** the voice becomes a genuine signal of category — "this is an EduNexus thing" becomes an instant, pre-conscious classification the moment the voice starts, independent of the specific content. Authority is now fully established; trust compounds because nothing has ever contradicted the pattern.

**One thousand times:** per the House Voice Standard's own Longevity Test (§14), a genuinely well-cast, disciplined voice at this frequency should feel less like a media presence and more like a known, trusted person in the listener's life — the same relationship a long-running national broadcaster's voice or a beloved children's-program host develops over a real career. **Fatigue risk at this frequency is low specifically because the voice was never exciting to begin with** — a voice cast for and evaluated against short-term charisma would show real fatigue and irritation risk by this point; a voice cast for calm, restrained consistency does not, because there is no performed energy to wear thin.

**Would it remain believable:** yes, more so — repetition without a single instance of overselling, alarm, or performance is itself the strongest possible evidence a listener could have that the voice is trustworthy, which is precisely why nothing in this casting process should compromise on any of the Rejection Rules in Section 8 for the sake of a candidate who is merely pleasant to listen to once.

## 6. Educational Suitability

Testing range across every register the House Voice Standard's own audience analysis (§3) requires — fractions, science, history, career guidance, parent advice, teacher professional development, research findings — without the narrator sounding like it "belongs" to any one of them.

The test is not whether the same words work across all seven; it is whether the *same person* is audibly present in all seven. A candidate who sounds like a children's-TV presenter on fractions and a different, more clinical person on research findings has failed this test even if each individual read sounds good in isolation — the whole premise of a single, permanent narrator (House Voice Standard §3) depends on one calm, curious, unhurried person being equally at home explaining a Grade 7 misconception and a Ministry-facing research finding, varying only vocabulary and pacing of *content*, never *register of person* (House Voice Standard §3's own explicit rule). Section 8's audition script is built specifically to test this range directly, in one sitting, so this isn't left to inference across separate recordings.

## 7. Emotional Range

Testing the seven registers named in the brief against the House Voice Standard's own Emotional Range table (§9):

| Test | Expected result | Failure sign |
|---|---|---|
| Asking a question | Genuine, unhurried curiosity; real question intonation | Game-show or quiz-host energy |
| Correcting a misconception | Gentler, slower, more space between clauses | Any sharpness, any note of correction-as-judgment |
| Revealing an insight | Plain, clear statement, no dramatized build | Suspense, a "big reveal" vocal shape |
| Celebrating understanding | Warmest point in the range, still fully controlled | Loud, exclamatory, performed excitement |
| Encouraging effort | Quiet, sincere, no inflation | Motivational-speaker energy, false enthusiasm |
| Explaining evidence | Measured, precise, deliberate on numbers and specifics | Rushed, or conversely over-dramatized delivery of data |
| Remaining silent | Comfortable, unforced stillness — no urge to fill the space | Audible discomfort, a rushed re-entry after the pause |

**The emotional limit that must hold across every one of these:** a listener should be able to identify the same person in all seven clips, played back to back, out of order — if any one register sounds like it belongs to a different narrator, that register is being *performed* rather than genuinely *inhabited*, and the candidate should be reconsidered regardless of how good any single clip sounds alone.

## 8. Candidate Rejection Rules

Immediate, non-negotiable rejection triggers — extending the House Voice Standard's forbidden-registers list (§10) into concrete audition red flags.

| Register | Rejection sign at audition | Why it damages educational trust |
|---|---|---|
| Movie trailer energy | Any building intensity, dramatic pacing shifts | Manufactures stakes the content doesn't have — see House Voice Standard §10 |
| Radio advertisement | Urgency, a "sell" quality even on neutral copy | Signals persuasion, not information |
| Motivational speaker | Inspirational lift at sentence ends, energy building toward a "point" | Implies certainty the evidence-first platform can't always offer |
| Preacher | Rhythmic, rising-intensity delivery, audience-address quality | Wildly wrong register for individual, evidence-grounded content |
| Politician | Quotable, applause-line cadence; evasive smoothness on nuance | Nuance and hedged confidence are core to this platform's honesty; this register can't hold them |
| Corporate training narrator | Flat, polished, impersonal — sounds like it belongs to no one specific | The opposite of the specific, real person this casting process exists to find |
| AI assistant | Bright, brisk, transactional neutrality | Undercuts the platform's entire differentiation from a generic assistant |
| YouTube influencer | High energy, reaction-forward, parasocial performance | Built for attention-competition, not trust-building |
| Sports commentator | Excitement escalation, real-time energy | Inappropriate for content that may concern a real child's struggle |
| Overly theatrical documentary narrator | "In a world..." vocal weight on ordinary information | Overstates stakes vocally rather than letting real stakes speak for themselves |

**A candidate triggering even one of these under audition pressure (Section 9) should be rejected outright, regardless of how strong their baseline conversational voiceprint (Section 3) was** — pressure-tested register failures are the single most reliable predictor of what a voice will drift toward under real production deadlines, per the Production Playbook's own finding that drift shows up first under pressure, not in calm conditions.

## 9. Audition Framework

**Length of reference recording per candidate:** a minimum of 10 minutes of varied material (Section 12's audition script plus unscripted conversation) — enough to evaluate consistency across registers (Section 6–7), not just a single pleasing clip. A separate, longer 20–30 minute session should follow for any candidate who passes this first round, both to stress-test long-form fatigue (House Voice Standard §11) and to gather sufficient clean audio for a technology evaluation pass (Section 10).

**Recording environment:** a quiet, acoustically treated or at minimum acoustically calm room — no requirement for a professional studio at the casting stage, but genuinely low background noise and no reverberant hard-surfaced room, since room character captured at this stage becomes difficult to separate from voice character later.

**Microphone quality:** a clean, reasonably neutral condenser or dynamic microphone appropriate for voice recording — broadcast-grade preferred but not mandatory at the casting stage; what matters most is consistency (the same microphone and chain across a candidate's entire audition) so later comparisons aren't confounded by equipment changes.

**Reading style:** natural, unhurried, exactly as if genuinely explaining the material to one specific person in the room, not "reading for a microphone" — candidates should be explicitly instructed to imagine they are speaking to one real learner, teacher, or parent they know, not performing for an audience.

**Number of takes:** at least two per passage — a first take (to capture the honest, un-self-conscious version) and a second (to check whether the candidate's *instinct* under a second attempt is to add performance, which is itself diagnostic — see Section 8's pressure-test principle) — never more than three, since additional takes increasingly capture rehearsed performance rather than a natural voiceprint.

**Emotional variations required:** all seven registers from Section 7, each read at least once.

**Passage types required:** all four passages from Section 12's audition script (math/curriculum, career/research register, parent-facing register, unscripted conversational baseline).

**Mistakes to avoid, explicitly briefed to every candidate before recording:** do not "perform" for the microphone; do not adopt a different voice or accent than natural conversational speech; do not rush to fill silences; do not try to sound "exciting" on any passage, including the celebration-register test.

**Minimum recording needed to evaluate a candidate fairly:** the 10-minute first-round session (Section 9) is sufficient for a genuine pass/fail decision against Sections 3, 6, 7, and 8; the 20–30 minute follow-up is required only for candidates who pass the first round and move toward Section 10's technology evaluation.

## 10. Technology Evaluation

Only now, against a real reference recording from a cast, approved human voice — not before, and not as a way of choosing the person.

| Technology | Can it faithfully reproduce a specific cast voice? | Compromises | Future-proofing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **XTTS (Coqui, open-source)** | Strong zero-shot voice cloning from a real reference sample; since it clones the specific speaker's identity (including their natural accent) rather than applying a generic accent model, a genuine Kenyan English reference should carry through faithfully | Prosody and fine emotional control are less refined than top commercial systems; occasional artifacts on longer continuous passages | High — open-source, self-hostable, no vendor dependency; the studio owns the pipeline outright |
| **F5-TTS (open-source)** | Strong zero-shot cloning quality, often praised for natural prosody in recent evaluation; a credible peer to XTTS | Newer, smaller ecosystem and community tooling; less production-hardened track record | High — same self-hosting advantage as XTTS; worth a direct side-by-side comparison against XTTS once a reference recording exists, rather than choosing on paper |
| **StyleTTS2 (open-source)** | High potential naturalness; style-diffusion approach can produce very convincing results | Historically more involved to fine-tune well for a specific new voice and a non-default accent; higher setup effort | High in principle (self-hostable), but the effort-to-result ratio needs a real, hands-on trial before recommending over XTTS/F5 |
| **ElevenLabs (commercial API)** | Very strong voice cloning quality out of the box, including a "Professional Voice Clone" tier with real emotional range control; likely the fastest path to a first high-quality test of the cast voice | Vendor lock-in — the studio does not own or control the underlying model, pricing, or roadmap; a direct tension with this document's own "must remain unmistakable for decades" mandate, since the studio's permanent asset would depend on a third party's continued existence and terms | Lower — genuinely useful as a fast validation tool (see recommendation below), riskier as the permanent, sole production pipeline for a twenty-year brand asset |
| **OpenVoice (MyShell, open-source)** | Good zero-shot cloning with flexible tone/style control, generally considered a notch behind XTTS/F5 on raw naturalness in recent comparative use, though it offers real advantages for controlling emotional register directly | Slightly less mature naturalness ceiling than the top open-source options as of this review | High — self-hostable; worth including in the same hands-on comparison round as XTTS/F5 given its emotional-control strengths map well onto Section 7's requirements |
| **Piper (open-source, lightweight)** | Fast, low-resource, works well offline and on modest hardware — but generally lower expressiveness and naturalness than the cloning-focused models above | Not well suited as the primary flagship narration voice; its real strength is a low-bandwidth, offline-capable *secondary* delivery mode, relevant given the platform's Kenyan classroom/connectivity context | Moderate — valuable as a future companion tool for constrained environments, not a candidate for the primary House Voice pipeline |
| **Commercial multi-voice APIs (Azure/Google/AWS neural voices, generally)** | Weak for this specific need — these are built around a catalog of pre-built voices, and faithfully cloning one specific cast individual typically requires a separate, higher-barrier custom-voice program with real approval overhead and cost | Not a good fit for a studio that has already cast a specific, real human voice it needs reproduced exactly | Lower for this use case specifically — better suited to a studio that hadn't cast a real person and was choosing from a catalog instead, which is explicitly not this studio's situation |

**Recommendation, technology following identity as mandated:** once a candidate is cast and a clean reference recording exists (Section 9), run the same short passage through XTTS, F5-TTS, and OpenVoice as the primary open-source, self-hostable comparison set — evaluated purely on fidelity to the real recording using this document's own Voiceprint Specification (Section 3) as the scoring rubric, not on general voice quality. ElevenLabs is worth a fast, low-cost parallel test specifically to establish a quality ceiling and sanity-check the open-source results against a strong commercial baseline — but the primary, long-term production pipeline should be the open-source option that scores highest against Section 3, specifically because owning the pipeline is itself a requirement of a genuinely twenty-year brand asset, not an optional preference. Piper should be evaluated separately, later, purely as a possible low-bandwidth companion mode, never as a substitute for the primary voice.

## 11. Final House Voice Casting Decision

This document does not itself name a cast individual — no audition has yet occurred, and per this brief's own mandate, technology and even the final casting decision must follow a real audition process (Section 9), not be declared in the abstract. What this section fixes, in place of a name, is the **decision criteria** the Editorial Standards Council must apply once real candidates have been auditioned:

1. Passes every item in Section 3's Voiceprint Specification in natural, unscripted conversation, not only in prepared reading.
2. Triggers zero items on Section 8's Rejection Rules, including under the pressure of a second take.
3. Is audibly the same person across all seven emotional registers in Section 7 and all four passage types in Section 12.
4. Produces a reference recording of sufficient length and quality (Section 9) to support a real technology fidelity test (Section 10).
5. Is not already a known public voice elsewhere, per Section 2's reasoning against imported associations.

A candidate meeting all five should be presented to the Council with the full audition recording, not a summary, for final sign-off — consistent with the Production Playbook's own standing rule that no gate is self-certified.

## 12. Audition Script

Four short passages, each testing a distinct register from Section 6, plus the unscripted conversational baseline required by Section 9. This is casting material — used to evaluate candidates, not production narration.

**Passage A — Curriculum/explaining register (tests the "explaining" and "correcting" emotional registers):**

> "Here's something most people get wrong at first. If you cut a cake into four equal pieces, each piece is bigger than if you'd cut the same cake into five. So three pieces from the four-piece cake — three quarters — is actually more than three pieces from the five-piece cake. It feels backwards, because five is a bigger number than four. But the denominator isn't telling you how much you have. It's telling you how finely the whole was divided."

**Passage B — Career/research register (tests the "introducing evidence" and broader-audience registers):**

> "Across the learners we've followed this term, one pattern kept showing up: the ones who returned to a difficult topic a second time, even briefly, retained far more than the ones who moved on immediately after getting it right once. That's not a guess. It's what the evidence, gathered across real classrooms, actually shows."

**Passage C — Parent-facing register (tests warmth, restraint, and the "correcting/concern" register at its most sensitive):**

> "You might notice a flag like this on your child's report and feel your stomach drop a little. That's completely understandable. But this isn't a verdict — it's a specific, evidenced observation about one area, at one point in time, and it comes with something you can actually do about it tonight."

**Passage D — Celebration/encouragement register (tests the upper warmth boundary without tipping into performance):**

> "Look at that. A month ago, this was the exact question this learner got wrong. Today, they walked through it without hesitating once. That's not luck. That's the work showing up."

**Unscripted baseline (required, no script provided to the candidate):** ask the candidate to explain, in their own words and with no preparation, how they would help a confused twelve-year-old understand why three quarters is bigger than three fifths — evaluated purely for the natural voiceprint (Section 3), independent of any writer's phrasing.

## 13. Recording Specification

- **Format:** uncompressed WAV, minimum 44.1kHz/16-bit (48kHz/24-bit preferred if equipment allows), mono.
- **Session length:** 10 minutes minimum for first-round candidates (Section 9); 20–30 minutes for finalists.
- **Environment:** quiet, low-reverberation room; no requirement for a commercial studio, but consistent conditions across a candidate's full session.
- **Chain consistency:** same microphone, same input gain, same room for a given candidate's entire audition — cross-candidate comparisons are only fair if each candidate's own recordings are internally consistent.
- **Noise floor:** as low as practically achievable; audible background noise (traffic, fans, echo) should disqualify a take for evaluation purposes, not just be tolerated.
- **Labelling:** every take clearly labelled by candidate, passage, and take number, preserved unedited — no take should be cleaned up, compressed, or processed before evaluation, since processing can mask exactly the natural qualities Section 3 is trying to assess.
- **Retention:** all raw audition audio retained indefinitely for any candidate who advances past first round, both for the eventual technology fidelity test (Section 10) and as a historical record of the casting decision, per Section 14's preservation mandate.

## 14. Long-Term Voice Preservation Strategy

The cast individual's real, raw reference audio — not any synthesized derivative of it — is the studio's actual permanent asset, and must be treated accordingly:

- **Raw, unprocessed reference recordings** (Section 13) are preserved in at least two independent storage locations, indefinitely, regardless of which synthesis technology is chosen or later changed.
- **The choice of synthesis technology (Section 10) is explicitly not permanent** — it should be re-evaluated against the same preserved reference recording and the same Section 3 rubric whenever a materially better open, self-hostable option emerges, without ever needing to re-cast or re-record the voice itself.
- **A written agreement with the cast individual** should cover the specific, ongoing use of their voice as a synthesis reference for the studio's productions, including how long that arrangement holds and what happens if it needs to end — a detail outside this document's scope but a real precondition before any reference recording is used in production, flagged here so it isn't silently skipped.
- **Periodic re-validation** (annually, or whenever a new production surfaces a Voice Gate finding per the Production Playbook's own routing discipline) should confirm the in-production synthesized voice still matches the original reference recording against Section 3's rubric — synthesis pipelines can drift with model updates even when the underlying reference audio hasn't changed, and this is the mechanism that catches it.
- **The reference recording, not any single production's rendered narration, is the source of truth** — if a future technology migration ever produces a result that sounds different from the original cast voice, the preserved reference recording is what any dispute or recalibration is checked against, never a prior production's output, which is already one synthesis step removed from the actual person cast.
