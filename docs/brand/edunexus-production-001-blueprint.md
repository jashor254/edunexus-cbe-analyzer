## 1. Executive Summary

Production #001 exists to permanently correct one specific, well-documented, high-frequency misconception — that a larger denominator means a larger fraction — using a mathematically clean pair (¾ and ⅗, sharing numerator 3) that isolates the misconception with no numerator interference. Every element of this production, mapped below, exists to move a real Grade 7 learner from that wrong belief to a portable, general rule: *more cuts to the same whole make each piece smaller*.

This blueprint's job is narrow: take everything already decided across six prior review passes and state, in one place, exactly how the Story Engine's Lifecycle, the House Voice, the Visual Language, and the Sonic Identity apply to this specific script — and name, honestly, the handful of items still open before this piece is actually ready to render. The studio should stop *re-deciding* things after this document. It should not stop *closing* the small number of things this document identifies as still open.

## 2. Educational Mission

**Why this production deserves to exist:** because the misconception it corrects — bigger denominator, bigger fraction — is not a knowledge gap most classroom teaching actually closes. As the Creative Development review established, standard instruction teaches the comparison *procedure* (common denominators, cross-multiplication) without ever forcing a learner to update the wrong belief underneath it. A learner can execute the procedure correctly and still privately believe the wrong thing, which resurfaces the moment the procedure isn't cued. This production exists specifically to repair the belief, not to re-teach a procedure most learners have already technically seen.

**Which misconception it corrects:** "a fraction with a larger denominator is a larger fraction" — tested here through ¾ vs. ⅗, a pair sharing the numerator 3, which strips away every other variable and isolates exactly the question this production answers: what does the denominator actually mean?

**Why it's worth the learner's time:** because the corrected understanding — the denominator states how finely the whole was divided — is not a fact about two specific fractions. It's the seed of correct reasoning about every fraction comparison the learner will ever do afterward, which is the entire justification the Story Engine's Transfer Test (§10) requires before any production earns its runtime.

## 3. Learning Contract

Distinguishing knowledge from genuine understanding, per the Story Engine's own ladder (§2).

| | Target |
|---|---|
| **Know** | That ¾ is bigger than ⅗, and that the reason involves how many pieces the whole was cut into |
| **Understand** | That a denominator describes partition count, not quantity — and that holding the numerator fixed, more cuts to the same whole produce smaller, not bigger, pieces (an inverse relationship, not a fact to memorize) |
| **Be able to explain** | Unprompted, in their own words: "more cuts to the same whole make each piece smaller" — the memory target itself, stated as a general rule, not tied to the specific numbers 3, 4, and 5 |
| **Be able to transfer** | Correctly compare any new same-numerator fraction pair (the script's own transfer test uses ⅔ vs. 2/7) by reasoning about partition size, not by recalling a memorized fact |
| **Still remain curious about** | What happens when the numerators are *different* — a question this production deliberately does not answer (per its own honestly bounded scope), left open as a natural next question rather than closed off or overclaimed |

**The distinction that matters most:** "know" alone is what most ordinary fraction instruction already produces, and per Section 2 above, it's exactly what doesn't survive contact with a differently-framed problem. This production is built to fail if a learner only reaches the "know" row.

## 4. Memory Target

**The single sentence meant to survive one week later:** *"More cuts to the same whole make each piece smaller."*

**A finding this blueprint must not paper over:** the Table Read identified a real, evidenced risk that this exact target is currently competing for recall against a shorter, more concrete line — "Three quarters is bigger" (script line 8) — which arrives earlier, is more concrete, and currently sits next to the strongest silent beat in the piece. The Decision Log's DL-009 entry is marked **"Under review"** for exactly this reason, and this blueprint is the place that tension gets a concrete, actionable resolution rather than remaining an open question indefinitely.

**Resolution directed here, at the blueprint level, without rewriting a single word of script text:** the *staging*, not the *wording*, changes. Specifically — carried forward into the Visual Blueprint (Section 6) and Voice Blueprint (Section 7) below — the silent pause and visual isolation currently given most strongly to line 8's reveal must be rebalanced so that line 13 (the actual memory target) receives the single strongest visual and silent treatment in the entire piece, not line 8. Line 8 remains a clear, satisfying moment; it must no longer be staged as *the* moment. This is a direction this blueprint is authorized to give without reopening the Table Read or the Script Lab, because it resolves a staging problem, not a wording problem.

## 5. Story Blueprint

The full ten-stage Lifecycle, mapped against the actual script, per stage — educational, emotional, and cognitive purpose stated for each.

| Stage | Script content | Educational purpose | Emotional purpose | Cognitive purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Orientation** | "Two fractions: three quarters, and three fifths." | Establish the exact objects being compared | Recognition — a concrete, ungeneralized starting point | Low load — orient before asking anything of the viewer |
| **Question** | "Which is bigger?" | Force a real, committed prediction | Genuine curiosity opens | Primes attention for what follows |
| **Expectation** | "Most people say three fifths — five is bigger than four, so it feels like more." *(flagged for split, Table Read §11)* | Surface the misconception as the viewer's own plausible reasoning, not a strawman | Normalizes the wrong guess as intelligent, not careless | Sets up the Gap that follows |
| **Observation** | The two wholes, cut into four and five pieces | Ground the coming contradiction in a checkable, concrete artifact | Attentiveness, evidence-forward, no editorializing | Medium load — the actual evidence being processed |
| **Gap** | "Three quarters is bigger." / "So how can three fifths be smaller?" | Make the contradiction explicit and personal | Surprise or confirmation-satisfaction | Highest attention peak in the piece (Story Engine §8) |
| **Investigation** | "Look at what changed... only the number of cuts is different." *(flagged for split, Table Read §11)* | Isolate the true variable — the mechanism, shown, not asserted | Patient interest | The piece's only High-load stage — protected, not rushed |
| **Recognition/Resolution** | "The denominator isn't how much you have. It's how finely the whole was divided." | Generalize the mechanism from this pair to denominators in general | Clarity, relief — no dramatized delivery | Consolidating the causal explanation |
| **Meaning** | "More cuts to the same whole make each piece smaller." | The memory target itself — restated as a standalone, portable rule | Quiet significance, not sentimentalized | The piece's actual deliverable — protected per Section 4 above |
| **Reflection** | The pause immediately following | Give the memory target room to settle before moving on | Calm | Consolidation — no new pattern introduced here, per Story Engine §5 |
| **Application/Transfer** | "Try it: two thirds, or two sevenths..." through the closing line | Test whether the mechanism generalized, not whether this specific pair was memorized | Quiet confidence | Durability is built here, in the final third — not at the Gap's excitement peak (Story Engine §8) |

## 6. Visual Blueprint

Sequence of visual ideas, without drawing anything — every decision justified against the Visual Language Standard.

- **Orientation and Question:** visual simplicity at its maximum — the two fractions named, nothing else on screen, generous negative space (Visual Language §6), no colour applied yet (per §9, colour is reserved for what's evidenced, and nothing has been evidenced yet).
- **Observation:** the two wholes appear using the existing `bucket()`/comparable partition primitives already present in the studio's Manim asset library (per the Readiness Audit's finding that these already exist and match the Visual Dictionary), cut into four and five equal pieces respectively, three shaded in each — built with the strict parallel construction the Script Lab's own audit already confirmed is the clearest pair of lines in the piece. **No motion during the shading itself** — pieces appear, they do not animate into existence with flourish, per the Visual Language Standard's rule that motion must map to real change (§5), and nothing is changing yet, only being shown.
- **The Gap moment (line 8):** stillness, not motion — both wholes held on screen, fully visible, in the strongest negative-space composition in the piece **except** — per Section 4's resolution — this moment's silent pause should be real but measured, not the longest pause in the production. This is the corrected staging: clear, satisfying, but visually secondary to what follows.
- **Investigation:** a highlight (Visual Language §4's single-highlight rule, never more than one active at once) isolates first the constant (the shared "three pieces taken"), then the variable (the differing cut count) — sequential, never simultaneous, directly implementing this Standard's own single-focal-point rule (§6).
- **Recognition/Resolution:** colour is introduced here for the first time — the confirmed-role colour (Visual Language §9) fills in once the mechanism has actually been evidenced, not before. This is the first moment in the entire piece color plays any role at all, which is deliberate: color earns its appearance the same way the memory sentence earns its statement.
- **Meaning (line 13) — the corrected staging point:** this is where the piece's strongest visual treatment now belongs. The two wholes return to frame, side by side, fully still, held in silence longer than any other moment in the production — the negative space here should be the most generous in the piece, and the pause accompanying it (Voice Blueprint, Section 7) the longest. This is the concrete fix directed in Section 4: line 13, not line 8, gets the piece's visual superlative.
- **Reflection:** true stillness — no new visual element introduced, matching the Story Engine's own rule against introducing new patterns at this stage (§5).
- **Application/Transfer:** the same visual grammar (two wholes, cut and shaded) repeats for the new pair (⅔ vs. 2/7), deliberately using the identical visual construction as the original comparison — repetition here is a feature, not a limitation, since it lets the viewer recognize the *pattern* of the visual reasoning, not just its specific content.
- **Excluded symbols, confirmed:** per the Readiness Audit and War Room, none of the non-admitted symbols (`funnel`, `sticky_note`, `graduation_cap`, `pencil_scribble`, `paper_fold_corner`, etc.) appear anywhere in this production.

## 7. Voice Blueprint

Applying the House Voice Standard to the actual script, incorporating the Table Read's editorial decisions directly.

- **Slow:** throughout — the entire script sits at the House Voice Standard's unhurried baseline (140–155 wpm); no section should be read faster than any other, including the transfer beat, where the Table Read specifically flagged pacing strain risk for a struggling-learner persona (§3).
- **Pause — corrected per Section 4:** the pause preceding line 13 becomes the longest deliberate silence in the entire recording, longer than the pause following the Observation reveal (line 8) — a direct, specific instruction reversing the current draft's implicit emphasis, without changing a single word.
- **Emphasize:** per the Script Lab's own audit (Section 4 of that document) — one stressed word per sentence at most, landing on meaning-bearing terms ("finely," "smaller," "same") never on adjectives for effect.
- **Remain neutral:** during the Observation lines (6–7) — plain, evidence-forward delivery, no editorializing tone, matching the "introducing evidence" register (House Voice Standard §9).
- **Allow silence:** at every pause marker already specified in the Script Lab, with the one adjustment already identified in the Table Read (§9 of that document) — the pause immediately before line 13 should run shorter than originally drafted **relative to its neighbors**, since Section 4's resolution moves the emphasis to line 13's own surrounding silence, not to the pause immediately preceding the mechanism statement in line 12.
- **Script edits still required before recording**, carried forward directly from the Table Read's Editorial Decisions (§11 of that document) and not yet applied: **split** lines 3, 10, and 14; **tighten** line 4 ("that's not careless") and line 9; resolve the still-open disagreement on line 16 (parent cue) via Editorial Standards Council ruling, not by default inclusion or exclusion.

## 8. Sonic Blueprint

The first real application of the Sonic Identity Standard to an actual production.

- **Where it begins:** a brief, restrained sonic mark at the very opening — before Orientation, marking the transition into the piece — a few seconds only, per the Sonic Identity Standard's own duration discipline (§4).
- **Where it ends:** the mirrored sonic mark at the close, after the final line, marking the transition out — never overlapping with the final spoken word.
- **Where silence intentionally replaces music, and why:** for the entire remainder of the production — every stage from Question through Application/Transfer runs with no musical bed of any kind. This is not a reduced or minimal score; per the Sonic Identity Standard's own Section 7, narration doing real explanatory work and music both compete for the same auditory channel, and this production's narration is doing real explanatory work in essentially every line. The evidence-reveal moments (lines 6–9, the two wholes and their contradiction) and the memory-target moment (line 13) are the two points in this production where the Sonic Identity Standard's rule against underscoring evidence or Reflection is most directly load-bearing — neither gets so much as a quiet pad.
- **Net result:** this production is sonically silent except for its opening and closing marks — a direct, concrete, first real test of whether the Sonic Identity Standard's restraint principle holds up in an actual production rather than remaining theoretical.

## 11. Post-Publication Evaluation Plan

Consolidated directly from the War Room's Section 11 and the Reference Production review's Section 7 — restated here as this production's actual, final plan, not a re-derivation.

- **Evidence collected:** a real, small-sample comprehension check with Grade 7 learners, both immediately after viewing and after a delay of several days — specifically distinguishing recall of the *specific fact* (¾ > ⅗) from recall of the *general mechanism* (the memory target), which directly tests whether Section 4's staging correction actually worked.
- **How misunderstanding will be detected:** verbatim recording of wrong answers on the transfer check (⅔ vs. 2/7), not just a pass/fail rate — the *kind* of error made is diagnostic of whether the Investigation stage's mechanism actually landed, per the Creative Development review's own methodology.
- **How future improvements will be decided:** any misconception discovered updates the Pattern Library's Correction pattern (P72) entry directly, per the Playbook's routing discipline (§13); any narration-pacing or Sonic Identity finding is logged and routed to the specific Standard it concerns, not just filed as a general production note.
- **How this becomes part of the Canon:** once published, reviewed, and its Post-Publication findings filed, this production is held permanently as the fixed reference point every future production is measured against — per the Reference Production review's own Section 8, it does not need to remain the studio's *best* piece to remain valuable as the *calibration* piece.
