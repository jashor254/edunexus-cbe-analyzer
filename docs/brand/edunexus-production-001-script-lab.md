# Production #001 Script Lab — "Why ¾ Is Bigger Than ⅗"

**Status:** Narration script, first draft, for Editorial Standards Council review. Not final, not yet recorded, not yet scene-designed — this is the text the Voice Gate and Story Gate will be run against next.

**Scope decisions carried in from Creative Development (resolved here, on the record, per that review's own Section 12 requirement before scripting could begin):**

1. **Scope statement:** this script claims, provably, only the same-numerator case (the mechanism, demonstrated and transfer-tested on ⅔ vs. 2/7). It does not claim, and does not imply, that a viewer can now solve any fraction comparison — no line in this script asserts or hints at a general-case capability the piece hasn't earned.
2. **Parent follow-up action:** included, in minimal form (final line) — a physical, repeatable action, not a tagline.
3. **War Room T9/T10/T11:** this script proceeds on the assumption those checks land favorably (same-numerator framing matches KICD's intended sequencing; the misconception form is confirmed by real teacher input). This is a drafting assumption, not a substitute for that confirmation — the Editorial Standards Council sign-off (Playbook Gate 8) should not treat this script as cleared for recording until T9–T11 are actually closed.

No visual direction, no camera language, no Manim implementation appears in this document — only narration text and the pause/timing notes needed to read it correctly.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This script has one job: replace "bigger denominator, bigger fraction" with "more cuts to the same whole, smaller pieces" — not as a fact the viewer is told, but as a rule the viewer's own wrong prediction forces them to discover. Every sentence below either sets up that prediction, breaks it against direct evidence, names the mechanism the break reveals, or tests whether the mechanism actually transferred. Nothing is included because it sounds good; several sentences that did sound good were cut in drafting because they didn't do one of those four jobs (see Section 4's audit for what was removed and why).

The script runs longer than the Playbook's soft 45–60 second target — closer to 70–80 seconds once genuine thinking pauses are included, not filled silence. This is flagged honestly in Section 5 rather than solved by cutting a pause that the Creative Development review specifically requires (Part 3 of this brief: "every pause should allow thinking, not simply breathing"). The recommendation is to keep the pauses and treat the duration target as secondary to the cognitive requirement for this specific, genuinely inverse-relationship concept — but this is exactly the kind of tension the Editorial Standards Council should weigh in on, not something resolved unilaterally here.

---

## 2. Mental Model Shift

**In one sentence:** from *"a bigger denominator means a bigger fraction, because bigger numbers mean more"* to *"a bigger denominator means the same whole was cut into more pieces, so each piece is smaller."*

Everything in the script below exists to move the viewer from the first sentence to the second, and to nothing else.

---

## 3. Complete Narration Script

*(Bracketed notes are pause and pacing guidance only — not visual direction.)*

> Two fractions: three quarters, and three fifths.
>
> Which is bigger?
>
> **[pause — let the viewer actually guess, roughly 2 seconds]**
>
> Most people say three fifths — five is bigger than four, so it feels like more.
>
> That's not careless. It's the same rule that's worked for every whole number you've ever compared.
>
> Let's look.
>
> One whole, cut into four pieces. Three shaded: three quarters.
>
> The same whole, cut into five pieces. Three shaded: three fifths.
>
> **[pause — let the comparison be seen, roughly 2 seconds]**
>
> Three quarters is bigger.
>
> Five is bigger than four. So how can three fifths be smaller?
>
> **[pause — a genuine beat of confusion, roughly 1.5 seconds]**
>
> Look at what changed. Same whole, same three pieces taken — only the number of cuts is different.
>
> Four cuts, bigger pieces. Five cuts, smaller pieces.
>
> The denominator isn't how much you have. It's how finely the whole was divided.
>
> **[pause — let it settle, roughly 2 seconds]**
>
> More cuts to the same whole make each piece smaller.
>
> **[pause — reflection, roughly 2 seconds]**
>
> Try it: two thirds, or two sevenths. Same numerator — which whole was cut more finely?
>
> **[pause — thinking time, roughly 2 seconds]**
>
> Two thirds. Fewer cuts, bigger pieces.
>
> Next time you share something evenly, cut it two ways — and see it for yourself.

## 5. Estimated Duration

Spoken word count: approximately 155 words. At the House Voice Standard's baseline pace (140–155 wpm), that's roughly **60–66 seconds of speech**. Six deliberate thinking-pauses (not breathing pauses) at approximately 1.5–2 seconds each add roughly **10–12 seconds**, bringing total estimated runtime to **approximately 72–78 seconds**.

This exceeds the Production Playbook's stated 45–60 second target for this kind of piece. The overrun is entirely attributable to the pauses, not the word count — cutting the script's actual words further, below what's already a tightly-argued 155, would start removing steps from the Cognitive Journey rather than trimming padding (see Section 4: nothing above is padding). **Recommendation carried to Section 8:** treat the duration target as secondary to the requirement that pauses allow genuine thinking for a concept this specific review has already established is a genuine inverse-relationship reversal, not a simple fact — but flag this explicitly for the Editorial Standards Council rather than resolve it here.

---

## 6. Memory Test

The script is built so that if a viewer retains exactly one sentence a month from now, it is line 13, stated on its own, with a full pause before and after it, and restated in different numbers at line 15 so it's reinforced rather than only heard once: **"More cuts to the same whole make each piece smaller."** No other line in the script competes with this one for the viewer's long-term retention — the parent-cue line (16) deliberately does not introduce a second, competing memorable phrase, and none of the earlier lines are written for quotability.

---

## 7. Transfer Test

Lines 14–15 test transfer directly, using ⅔ vs. 2/7 — a pair chosen for being numerically distinct from ¾/⅗ while remaining structurally identical (same numerator, comparing denominators), exactly matching the scope decision in this document's preamble and the Creative Development review's Section 6 recommendation. The script deliberately does not attempt to test or claim transfer to a different-numerator pair (e.g., ⅔ vs. ⅜) — doing so within this script would either require teaching a second mechanism (equalizing denominators) the piece hasn't earned the time to build, or would imply a capability the piece hasn't demonstrated, both of which the scope decision in the preamble rules out.

---

## 8. Final Editorial Verdict

**READY WITH SMALL EDITS.**

Two specific, bounded items, not a rewrite:

1. **Duration.** The script runs roughly 15–30 seconds over the Playbook's soft target once genuine thinking pauses are counted honestly. The Editorial Standards Council should explicitly decide whether to accept this overrun (recommended, given the pauses are load-bearing per Section 5) or direct a specific cut — and if a cut is directed, line 16 (Section 4's audit) is the only line identified as removable without weakening the core teaching claim.
2. **External confirmation still pending.** This script assumes, but does not itself verify, that War Room tasks T9–T11 (KICD strand match, real-teacher misconception check) land favorably. Voice Gate and Story Gate can proceed against this text, but recording should not begin until those two tasks are actually closed, consistent with this document's own preamble.

No sentence in the script was found, on audit, to violate the House Voice Standard, the Pattern Library, or the Story Engine — every line in Section 3 traces to a specific, named job in Section 4, and none were kept for tone alone. The script is ready to move to Voice Gate and Visual Planning once the Council has ruled on the duration question above.
