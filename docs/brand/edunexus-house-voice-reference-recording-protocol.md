# The EduNexus House Voice — Reference Recording Session Protocol

**Status:** Recording protocol only. No voice is evaluated, scored, cast, or compared here. This document exists to produce the evidence — nothing else — that every future House Voice review, stress test, and technology comparison will run against. Until a recording following this protocol exists, no casting decision has sufficient evidence behind it, per the [Casting Document](edunexus-house-voice-casting.md)'s own standing rule.

**Relationship to prior work:** the Casting Document's Section 9 (Audition Framework) and Section 12 (Audition Script) already specified a first-round, 10-minute audition. This protocol is the deeper, second-round session that document explicitly requires before any candidate can be evaluated as a finalist (§9: "a separate, longer 20–30 minute session should follow for any candidate who passes this first round"). It expands four short passages into six purpose-built ones, adds a stability check the first round didn't require, and adds the archival standard that makes the result a permanent institutional asset rather than a one-off file.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Every evaluation attempted so far in this casting process has stalled on the same problem: insufficient evidence. A 16-second preview clip cannot support a House Voice verdict, and it cannot support a stress test across curriculum, parent, teacher, career, long-form, and short-form registers — not because the reviewer lacks judgment, but because the material itself was never designed to carry that weight. This protocol exists to close that gap permanently, once, with a single well-designed session, rather than accumulating more short, purpose-mismatched clips that each individually fall short of what a real evaluation requires.

The session below runs 20–30 minutes across six passages, each engineered to reveal a specific, named quality the House Voice Standard requires, plus a stability check that repeats two passages at the end of the session to catch fatigue or drift a single pass-through would miss. The result, properly archived, becomes the one recording every future review — House Voice Review, Stress Test, technology fidelity comparison — draws from, rather than each evaluation improvising its own insufficient evidence base.

## 2. Recording Environment Specification

Extending the Casting Document's Section 13 baseline with the added rigor a permanent reference asset requires.

| Requirement | Specification | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Room** | A quiet, acoustically calm space — soft furnishings, curtains, or a small treated room preferred; avoid hard-surfaced, reverberant rooms (tiled kitchens, empty rooms, bathrooms) | Room character captured now becomes inseparable from the voice later; a reference recording carries its room forever |
| **Microphone placement** | 15–20cm from the candidate's mouth, slightly off-axis (not directly in front of the mouth) to reduce plosives | Standard voice-recording distance that captures natural warmth and resonance without excessive proximity effect or breath pops |
| **Distance consistency** | Fixed for the entire session — mark the chair and mic stand position physically (tape on the floor) so the candidate can't drift closer or further across a 20–30 minute session | A reference recording must be internally consistent, or later comparisons (Section 6) can't distinguish real vocal drift from accidental distance changes |
| **Sample rate / bit depth** | 48kHz / 24-bit | Higher than the Casting Document's stated minimum (44.1kHz/16-bit) — this session is the permanent master, not a first-round screen, and should be captured at archival quality from the start rather than upgraded later |
| **Format** | Uncompressed WAV, mono | No lossy compression at any point in the capture chain — compression artifacts, once baked in, can't be removed later |
| **Noise requirements** | Noise floor as close to silent as practically achievable; no fans, no traffic noise, no hum; a genuinely quiet space is a hard requirement for this session, not a preference | This recording will be used for cloning-fidelity technology comparisons later (Casting Document §10), which are especially sensitive to background noise |
| **Monitoring** | Closed-back headphones on the engineer, real-time level monitoring, peak levels kept comfortably below clipping (target -12dB to -6dB peak) | Clipping is unrecoverable; conservative gain staging protects the entire session |
| **Pop filter** | Required | Standard protection against plosive artifacts on "p," "b," "t" sounds, especially relevant given several passages below open with these sounds |
| **Session monitoring discipline** | Engineer checks playback after Passage A specifically, before continuing — not just at the end | Catches a bad setup (wrong gain, clipping, unexpected noise) after two minutes of loss, not after thirty |

## 3. Complete Session Plan

Target total runtime: 20–30 minutes. Six core passages, an unscripted passage, and a stability check — sequenced deliberately, not in an arbitrary order.

```
0. Room tone (30 seconds silence, mic live, nobody speaking)
1. Passage A — Curriculum explanation                  (~3 min)
2. Passage B — Educational Intelligence / evidence      (~3 min)
3. Passage C — Parent communication                     (~3 min)
4. Passage D — Teacher communication                     (~3 min)
5. Passage E — Career guidance                            (~3 min)
6. Passage F — Unscripted explanation (2 min, no script) (~2–3 min)
7. Short break (2–3 minutes, mic off, candidate rests)
8. Stability check — repeat Passage A                    (~3 min)
9. Stability check — repeat Passage C                     (~3 min)
10. Room tone (30 seconds silence, mic live)
```

**Why this order:** Passage A opens on the candidate's most naturally comfortable register (teaching a concept, the thing they're actually being cast to do), rather than opening on the hardest register (Passage C's emotional sensitivity) while the candidate is still warming up. Passage F (unscripted) is placed after all five scripted passages specifically so the candidate has already settled into the space and isn't performing a first-impression version of themselves — the goal is their authentic voice, not their most self-conscious one. The stability checks close the session, after a real break, specifically to test whether the same passages hold up once genuine session fatigue has had a chance to set in.

**Room tone requirement:** thirty seconds of pure silence at the start and end, mic live — a standard audio-engineering practice that captures the room's actual noise floor for later reference and gives any future technical processing a clean noise sample if ever needed, without altering the archived voice recording itself.

## 4. Passage Objectives

Six purpose-built passages — the actual text is provided so the session can be run immediately, not designed separately later.

**Passage A — Curriculum explanation** *(reveals: clarity, teaching rhythm, concept explanation, thinking pauses)*

> "Here's something that confuses a lot of people at first. Zero point five and one half look like completely different things — one's a decimal, one's a fraction. But they're the same number. If you cut something into two equal pieces and take one, that's a half. Write that same amount as a decimal, and it's zero point five. Different way of writing it. Same amount."

**Passage B — Educational Intelligence / evidence-based idea** *(reveals: authority, research communication, precision)*

> "There's a pattern that shows up again and again in how people actually learn. Testing yourself on something — even getting it wrong — builds stronger memory than reading the same material over and over. It feels less comfortable in the moment. But the evidence, across a lot of different classrooms and a lot of different subjects, points the same direction, consistently."

**Passage C — Parent communication** *(reveals: warmth, empathy, trust)*

> "I want to talk you through something in your child's report, because I know a flag like this can be worrying to see. This isn't a judgment on your child, and it isn't permanent. It's a specific, evidenced observation about one area, at one point in time — and it comes with something real you can do about it, starting tonight."

**Passage D — Teacher communication** *(reveals: respect, collaboration, professional credibility)*

> "Looking at this assessment pattern together — I don't think this is a motivation problem. The errors cluster specifically around one type of question, not across the board. That usually points to a gap in one particular skill, not a gap in effort. Worth checking whether that lines up with what you're seeing in class."

**Passage E — Career guidance** *(reveals: encouragement, hope, quiet confidence)*

> "You've been building something here that doesn't show up in one single grade — a habit of coming back to hard problems instead of walking away from them. That's not a small thing. That's the exact thing that ends up mattering most, years from now, long after this specific topic is behind you."

**Passage F — Unscripted explanation** *(reveals: the real person behind the prepared reading)*

> No script provided. Instruction to the candidate: "Pick any simple educational idea you understand well — from school, from teaching, from your own life — and explain it out loud, naturally, for about two minutes, as if you're explaining it to someone you know. No preparation, no notes."

## 5. Recording Checklist

Run in order, before, during, and after the session.

**Before the session:**
- [ ] Room checked for background noise (HVAC, traffic, other people) at the actual time of day recording will happen
- [ ] Microphone, pop filter, and headphones tested with a 30-second level check
- [ ] Chair and mic stand positions marked
- [ ] Candidate briefed per the Casting Document's explicit instruction: do not perform, do not adopt a different voice, do not rush to fill silences, do not try to sound "exciting" — read naturally, as if genuinely explaining to one real person
- [ ] Candidate given Passages A–E in advance to read silently once, but not rehearsed aloud multiple times (per Casting Document §9: rehearsed performance is a less reliable signal than a natural first or second take)

**During the session:**
- [ ] Room tone captured (30 seconds, start)
- [ ] Each passage recorded as its own separate file, clearly logged in order
- [ ] Playback check after Passage A specifically (Section 2)
- [ ] No more than two takes per scripted passage, per Casting Document §9's own rule against over-rehearsed later takes
- [ ] Passage F recorded with zero script, zero notes, genuinely unprepared
- [ ] Real break enforced before the stability checks — not skipped for time
- [ ] Room tone captured (30 seconds, end)

**Immediately after the session:**
- [ ] All files backed up to a second location before the candidate or engineer leaves the room
- [ ] A quick full playback of every file to confirm no corrupted or silent recordings
- [ ] Session logged per Section 7's naming and metadata standard, same day

## 6. Consistency Checks

The stability check (Passages A and C, repeated after a break) exists specifically to answer questions a single pass-through cannot:

- **Does pace change?** Compare words-per-minute between the original and repeated take of Passage A — a meaningful slowdown or speedup between the two suggests either fatigue or the candidate settling into a different, possibly less natural rhythm once "warmed up."
- **Does energy drop?** Compare the repeated Passage A and C takes for any flattening of the warmth and engagement present in the originals — energy dropping specifically on the repeat, after a real break, is a genuine signal about sustained-session viability, not just a bad single take.
- **Does pronunciation shift?** Listen specifically for any words pronounced differently between the original and repeat — inconsistency here, more than in any other measure, indicates the candidate hasn't yet settled into a fully natural, repeatable delivery.
- **Does warmth disappear?** Passage C specifically, repeated — this is the most emotionally demanding passage in the set, and the most likely to show genuine fatigue if it exists; a candidate whose warmth in the parent-communication register holds up on a tired, second attempt is showing real, not performed, quality.
- **Does fatigue appear at all?** The honest possible outcomes here are not just pass/fail — a candidate showing mild, specific fatigue only on the repeat of the hardest passage (C) is different information than one showing fatigue across the board, and both are more useful findings than a session with no repeat check at all would have produced.

**What this check cannot do:** predict genuine long-form fatigue across a 30-minute or 2-hour continuous session (Section 8 addresses this gap directly) — a short repeat after a short break tests short-term consistency, not true long-form endurance.

## 7. Archive Standard

This recording is a permanent institutional asset from the moment it's captured — treated accordingly, not as a working file that happens to survive.

**File naming convention:**
```
edunexus-housevoice-candidate[NN]-[passage]-[take]-[YYYYMMDD].wav

Examples:
edunexus-housevoice-candidate01-passageA-take1-20260805.wav
edunexus-housevoice-candidate01-passageC-repeat-take1-20260805.wav
edunexus-housevoice-candidate01-roomtone-start-20260805.wav
```

**Metadata (embedded in file + companion sidecar text file per session):**
- Candidate ID
- Session date and location
- Microphone model and audio interface used
- Sample rate / bit depth / format
- Engineer name
- Passage identifier and take number
- Any notable conditions (background noise events, retakes, candidate notes)

**Versioning:** raw files are never overwritten or edited in place — any processed or edited derivative (noise reduction, normalization, trimming) is saved as a new file with a `-processed` suffix, leaving the original raw capture untouched permanently, directly enforcing the Casting Document's own rule (§14) that the raw reference recording, not any processed derivative, is the actual source of truth.

**Backups:** at least two independent storage locations, confirmed working by an actual test restore (not assumed) — following the same discipline already established as a real, confirmed fix in the Production War Room for the studio's separate rendering pipeline.

**Master copy vs. working copy:** the master copy (raw, unprocessed, full session) is stored read-only, touched only for backup verification; a working copy is duplicated out for any actual evaluation, editing, or technology-comparison use, so the master is never at risk of accidental modification during real casting work.

**Checksum verification:** a checksum (e.g., SHA-256) generated for every raw file at the moment of archiving, stored alongside the metadata sidecar — enabling a future integrity check to confirm a master file hasn't been silently corrupted or altered, at any point, no matter how far in the future it's checked.

**Future migration policy:** whenever the studio's storage systems change (a new backup provider, a new file format standard, a new archival medium), the master files are migrated with their checksums re-verified before the old copies are retired — this recording should be readable and verifiably unaltered decades from now, independent of what storage technology the studio happens to be using at any given point.

## 9. Final Readiness Verdict

This session, run exactly as specified, produces **sufficient evidence for a full House Voice Review and for a technology fidelity comparison — but not yet sufficient evidence for the complete Stress Test previously attempted.**

**What remains missing, stated exactly, per Part 11's instruction:**

1. **A genuine long-form passage** — one continuous, unbroken 15–20 minute read (not six short passages totaling that length) is required before any claim about 10-minute-lesson, 30-minute-documentary, or 2-hour-audiobook fatigue can be evidenced rather than guessed at.
2. **Genuine short-form passages** — one or more 15–60 second reads, written and paced the way real short-form production content actually is, before any claim about TikTok/Shorts/Reels suitability can be evidenced.

Both gaps are small, bounded additions to this same protocol — not a redesign — and could reasonably be appended as Passage G (long-form) and Passage H (short-form) in a second recording session, following this exact environment and archiving standard, once the studio is ready to close them.

Until this session, or an equivalent one, actually exists as a real, archived recording — not a plan for one — no House Voice Review, Stress Test, or casting verdict has sufficient evidence behind it. This document produces the protocol. It does not, and should not, produce the recording itself.
